# Guild community roads

Guild leaders and architects use **Guild roads** to mark a start at their feet, walk to the endpoint, preview the route, then confirm it within 90 seconds. The preview shows every affected cell and both endpoints. Removal has its own preview and restores the saved ground beneath the whole segment. Both actions recheck current membership and `housing.road.manage`; a stale menu does not retain guild authority.

Building on another member's personal plot requires that owner to grant an explicit road easement. While standing anywhere in their attached plot, the owner can open **Guild roads** and confirm **Grant** even without road-management permission. Consent is persisted for that exact property and attached guild; active membership, attachment and consent are rechecked when a route is committed. The owner's own route and the guild estate do not need another easement. The owner can remove intersecting road segments from this menu, then confirm **Revoke**; revocation is rejected while an intersecting segment still exists. Removing a segment restores the complete segment, including portions outside the owner's plot.

The first implementation builds straight, one-block-wide, level segments of 2–128 blocks. Both endpoints must lie inside active plots belonging to the guild community: the guild estate or an attached plot whose owner is still a member. Between those plots, at most five consecutive unclaimed cells may be crossed. Routes cannot cross unrelated plots, public roads, portals, another road, placed props or paths. They stay five blocks from houses and additions. Every cell needs loaded, natural, plain ground and two clear, dry blocks above it; containers, multiblocks, liquids and uneven ground are rejected. Build longer or turning routes as separate nonoverlapping segments.

Roads are guild-owned infrastructure and never become public or guild claim anchors. Their entire vertical column is protected; housing structures apply the same five-block road setback. Public roads and portals remain administrator-owned. This tool does not generate the public connector to a guild estate.

The free style is `cobblestone`, using the existing `Server/EterniaMod/PathStyles` catalog. Additional styles are available when the guild owns `eternia:path/<styleId>`. Add a registered path-style definition with a plain cube block, restart, and grant the guild its style entitlement through the existing ownership channel. Catalog path styles are shared with personal paths; road geometry and custody remain separate.

New plot claims and restored plots may not intersect an existing guild road. Remove that segment, claim the plot, then rebuild it. Public-road overlap remains supported. This avoids capturing a plot baseline without guild-road columns that may be removed later. Before packing a member plot, the adapter removes every intersecting road segment in full. Before packing the guild estate, it removes all the guild's segments in that world. This also runs for the 48-hour forced departure workflow. Already removed roads stay removed if a later plot-packing check fails. A failed or unresolved road removal prevents packing rather than losing the terrain snapshot.

After the intersecting roads are removed, pre-pack cleanup revokes that personal property's easement. The owner must explicitly grant consent again at the new location. If a later packing check fails, completed road removals and this revocation remain in effect.

`GuildRoads.startup(plugin)` loads the registry before protection is used. `protectedColumn(world,x,z)` and `roadRects(world)` expose active and unresolved segments; callers must not use them as anchors. `close()` stops preview timers. `open(ref,store,player)` opens the native menu, and `beforePack(world,plot)` runs on the world thread before plot baseline comparison.

Each road has immutable native before/after snapshots, verified SHA-256 references, a domain journal operation and a registry entry below `guild-roads`. Registry generations are immutable; the head is written with file flush, atomic replacement and readback. Pending protection is persisted before native writes. World writes are verified and flushed before the registry acknowledges success. A narrow world-thread scope bypasses only the road's exact segment during capture/apply/verify; public infrastructure is always protected. Startup reconciles unfinished journal manifests into protected recovery entries. A missing or corrupt registry head never resets the road list.

An interrupted segment appears with **Recover**. Recovery restores the saved original terrain only when every current cell matches the recorded before or after state, verifies and flushes it, removes the road and cancels its journal. An unexpected block, fluid or public infrastructure change keeps the operation unresolved for operator review. Retain all snapshot files, registry generations and `.pending` files; deleting them is not recovery.

Startup accepts an unfinished removal journal whose identical road is still recorded under its completed creation operation; that is the expected boundary before the removal registry write. If the final journal acknowledgement fails after terrain restoration, emergency recovery protection remains in memory even if the registry repair also fails. The segment cannot become unprotected merely because its registry removal was written first.

Unit tests cover endpoint/gap limits, exact setback boundaries, forbidden columns, route bounds, persisted pending/removal states, missing-head rejection, tampered generations, easement persistence and both journal/registry failure boundaries. The local Hytale acceptance fixture passed on 2026-09-12: a real guild road was built, removed and rebuilt; absent and revoked owner consent were rejected; forced packing removed an intersecting member segment and verified exact saved terrain while preserving separate guild decoration custody. Process termination and fresh-server recovery at each journal transition still require qualification before public use.
